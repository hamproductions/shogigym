# Lesson curriculum

This plan governs the existing lesson units and strategy pages. Teach a playable idea before asking learners to memorize a line. A lesson progresses from a named goal and an understandable position to an explained continuation, an opponent's alternative, and a check of understanding. An opening tutorial starts with the initial position; a tactical position is independently composed or derived from a documented game sequence. Label cooperative or deliberately weak replies.

## Learning path

1. **Rules:** setup and piece movement, then promotion and drops, check and mate, illegal moves and game endings. Finish by identifying legal moves, responding to check and completing a game.
2. **Playing a game:** material and exchanges, attack and defence, basic mating language and one-move mates. Introduce plans in the opening, middlegame and endgame, then forks and bishop exchanges. The learner can explain a threat and choose a response.
3. **First strategy:** learn why the king needs shelter, then choose one of Climbing Silver, Right Fourth File Rook, Fourth File Rook or Central Rook. The selected tutorial builds its formation from move one, explains an attack and an opponent's response, and connects to the matching practice lines. These are alternatives, not four mandatory consecutive strategies.
4. **Piece tactics:** pawn, lance, knight, silver, gold, bishop and rook techniques, followed by the two relevant proverbs. Each named technique needs a concrete example, legal continuation, defence or counterexample, and an identification cue.
5. **Castles and attacks:** build the common castles before studying their weaknesses. Teach attack by direction and target, including opponent defences, without promising a forced result where the position does not establish one.
6. **Endgame:** three-move mates and mating techniques, mating attacks, threats and defence, then five-move mates and position judgement. For each mate verify all legal defences and state the piece-in-hand convention. A five-move problem must exclude a shorter forced mate at its start.
7. **Develop a repertoire:** choose a strategy page, study its introductory tutorial, then related plans, opposing systems and practice lines. Other strategy pages are optional alternatives, not an automatic required sequence.
8. **Advanced systems:** study Bishop Exchange, Double Wing Attack, Side Pawn Capture, Fujii System, Bishop-Exchange Ranging Rook and Double Ranging Rook according to interest. Handicap study remains a separate practical option. Professional opening patterns do not by themselves confer professional playing strength.

## Revision and acceptance

- Inventory all planned lessons, sources, current outputs and replacements before rewriting a collection; use `remaining-inventory.json` for the outstanding non-opening units.
- Preserve standard opening facts and legal game sequences while writing original instruction, choosing original organization and composing original teaching positions. References are consultation only, with `adapted: false`; do not copy a source's composed position or collection structure.
- Complete units sequentially. Check legal replay, both sides' intentions, movement and captures, promotion, piece inventory and annotations. Check engine evaluations where applicable; shallow engine results do not establish forced mate or best play.
- Generate the lesson catalog from the authored units, run the project-required unit/course and originality checks, and verify the actual lesson experience and practice links in the permitted runtime. Record separately what has and has not been verified.
